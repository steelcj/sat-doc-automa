# How to judge things that promise wellbeing, in plain language

Version: 0.1.0
Status: Draft
Full version: universal-cake-wellbeing-evaluation-metrics-v0.3.0

## Who this is for

This is the easy-reading version of our wellbeing checklist. It says the same things as the full version, in everyday words. If the two versions ever seem to disagree, the full version is the official one.

## What this checklist is for

The world is full of things that promise to make you well. Meditation apps. Fitness programs. Online courses. Retreats. Diets. Habits from books and videos. Some are wonderful. Some are useless. Some do real harm.

This checklist helps you tell them apart. You can use it on anything that makes a wellbeing promise. We call the thing you are checking "the subject."

## How to rate things

For each question, give one of four answers:

- **Strong** — the subject really helps here.
- **Moderate** — it is okay, but has real weak spots. Name them.
- **Weak** — it actually works against you here.
- **Unknown** — you cannot tell yet. Write down what would help you find out.

Then say how you know:

- **Verified** — you checked it yourself, or solid research backs it up.
- **Inferred** — you have not tested it, but good clues point that way.
- **Claimed** — the seller says so. That is all you have.

That last part matters a lot. Wellness products make big claims. Most are never checked by anyone. A "Strong" that is only Claimed is worth less than a "Moderate" you Verified yourself.

## Deal-breakers

A few problems are so serious that nothing else can make up for them. If you find one, stop. Do not average it away with good points. These are the deal-breakers:

- **It hides its harms.** Every real practice can hurt someone, somehow. Honest ones say who and how. If a subject claims it is safe for absolutely everyone, walk away.
- **It locks people out.** If people with disabilities, low incomes, or no special training cannot use it at all, and there is no adapted way in, that is a failure.
- **There is no way to leave.** If quitting costs you your progress, your records, or your own data, you are a hostage, not a customer.
- **Its tricks cannot be turned off.** Streaks, fake urgency, endless feeds — in a product that claims to serve your wellbeing? If you cannot switch those off, the product's real goal is not your wellbeing.

## The one big question

If you only remember one thing from this page, remember this question:

> **If this thing disappeared tomorrow, what would you keep?**

A good practice teaches you skills that live in you. A meditation app should leave you able to meditate without it. A fitness program should leave you knowing how to move. If everything you gained vanishes when the product does, the product built a need, not a skill.

## What to check

**The ground it stands on.** Does it protect your sleep, or steal from it? Does it fit in your real life, or demand time you do not have? Can you afford it at the level that actually works — not just the free trial? Do you feel safe to make mistakes and ask questions?

**Your body.** Does it work with your real body, or an imagined perfect one? Does it make food a source of guilt? Does it treat rest as part of the plan, or as failure? "Push through the pain" is a warning sign, not a motto.

**Your mind.** Does it help you focus, or chop your attention into bits? Are the goals yours, or made up for you — streaks, badges, leaderboards? Are you learning something real, or just keeping a counter alive?

**Other people.** Does it bring you closer to people, or replace them with a screen? Does its community accept you as you are, or only if you pay and conform? Can you disagree with the teacher and still belong? What does your practice cost the people who live with you?

**Being here.** Through all of it, ask: does this thing help me actually be present — in my body, with my thoughts, with my people? Or does it keep me distracted while promising the opposite?

**The skills it leaves you.** For each skill — presence, handling feelings, self-kindness, resting — ask two things. Does the subject use the skill while you practise? And do you walk away with more of it in the rest of your life?

**The seller itself.** Can everyone get in — different bodies, incomes, languages? Is the pricing clear, or does it grow in the dark? Is the teaching shared, or dripped out behind paywall after paywall? Is there real evidence, or just a beautiful story? "It failed because you didn't believe" means the claim can never be tested — treat that as a red flag. And always: can you stop, keep your records, and leave whole?

## One more step: how far does it reach?

After the checks, write a few honest sentences: what does this subject actually affect, and how far does the effect travel?

Some fixes travel far. Better sleep improves your mood, focus, energy, and patience — one fix, many gains. Some fixes are narrow but still worthwhile. Both are fine. What is not fine is a subject that helps in one small place while quietly damaging the ground — like a fitness program that wrecks your sleep. Damage to the ground spreads to everything standing on it.

## We test ourselves too

This checklist judges other people's products, so it must pass its own test. That is why this easy-reading version exists, why we are working on French and Spanish, and why we admit what we have not verified yet. If this page is hard to follow, that is our failure, and we want to hear about it.
